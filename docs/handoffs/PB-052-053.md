# PB-052 and PB-053 — the walled-in pet, and the rock hex with no rock on it

*Written 1 August 2026 by the manager run that shipped them. Read this if you
have never seen this repo; it assumes nothing.*

---

## What this island is, in one paragraph

Juno's Island is a 3D toy island a six-year-old grows by answering reading and
maths questions. She spends progress on TILES, which are hexagons, and on EGGS,
which hatch into cube animals that wander about on the tiles. One kind of tile is
**rock**, which grows a mountain. The island's shape is hers and is saved; the
scenery on it is not saved at all but is **derived deterministically from the hex
coordinate**, so the same hex grows the same peak, facing the same way, on every
machine and after every reload. Brief §19 governs everything: *nothing a child
owns is ever lost.*

Two facts you need before either card makes sense:

- Adjacent hex centres are **exactly 2.0000 world units** apart.
- A mountain is measured two different ways, and both numbers are real:
  `footprintOf` (an axis-aligned half-extent, what PLACEMENT tests against) and
  `footprintBelow` (a rotation-invariant reach at walking height, what PETS
  collide against). They live in `src/island/world/mountains.ts` as
  `MOUNTAIN_FOOTPRINT` and `MOUNTAIN_KEEPOUT`, re-measured off the real `.gltf`
  files by `tests/island/mountains.test.ts`.

The eight mountain models split into two families by width:

| family | models | `footprintOf` | `MOUNTAIN_KEEPOUT` | weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A/B | `mountain_A`, `mountain_B`, `mountain_A_grass`, `mountain_B_grass`, `mountain_A_grass_trees` | 0.938085 | 1.038811 (1.062458 for `_A_grass_trees`) | 13/21 |
| C | `mountain_C`, `mountain_C_grass`, `mountain_C_grass_trees` | 1.011493 | 1.026901 | 8/21 |

Everything below follows from `2 x 1.011493 = 2.022986 > 2.0000` and
`2 x 0.938085 = 1.876170 < 2.0000`.

## PB-053 — one rock hex in seven had no mountain on it, permanently

### The defect

`src/island/world/props.ts` dresses tiles one at a time, keeping a running list
of `footprints` that new scenery must not stand inside. A tree gets six candidate
positions scattered around its hex. **A mountain gets one**, dead centre, because
the mound IS the tile (`spread = 0`). So when two adjacent rock hexes both drew a
C-family model, the second one stood inside the first, `firstClear` returned
null, and the code did `placed.add(k); continue` — marking the hex done with
nothing on it. `placed` is never cleared for the life of the session, so the hex
stayed bare. **Measured: 2763 of 19182 adjacent rock pairs, 14.4%.** 8/21 squared
is 14.51%, which is exactly where that number comes from.

### The fix

**A refused mountain is offered a narrower mountain, not a bare hex.**

- `mountains.ts` gained `MOUNTAIN_FALLBACKS` and `mountainFallbackFor(a)`. The
  narrow list is **derived from the measured footprint table, not written out by
  name**: a model qualifies when its radius plus the widest radius in the table
  still fits in the 2.0000 gap. Today that is exactly the five A/B models.
- `props.ts`, at the refusal point only and **only for a rock tile**, loads
  `mountainFallbackFor(a)` and re-runs `firstClear`. Same hex centre, native
  size, `mountainSpinFor(a)` facing, no blob shadow — the identical rock path,
  just a different file. If the fallback fails too, the hex is given up exactly
  as before.
- The retry sits **before anything is added to `group`**. That is load-bearing:
  a hex is marked `placed` before anything can throw so a re-sync cannot stack a
  second copy of a partly-dressed tile. Retrying the LOAD is only safe while
  nothing has been added yet.

**Result: 2763 refusals before, 2763 refusals and 0 bare hexes after.**

### Why the obvious fixes are wrong — do not retry them

1. **Do not move a constant.** `docs/HANDOFF.md:464` says so in as many words.
   The two mountains genuinely do overlap; the tolerance is not the problem.
2. **Do not swap `footprintOf` for `footprintBelow` at the placement test.** It
   was tried and measured. It refuses *every* adjacent rock pair and generalises
   this bug from 14% to **100%**.
3. **Do not touch `MOUNTAIN_HEXES`, its weights, or `hash`/`pick`.** The primary
   chooser is untouched by this fix on purpose: every mountain standing on an
   existing save stands in the same place, at the same size, facing the same way,
   afterwards. Only hexes that were BARE change. That is the PB-009 trap (scenery
   re-rolled on an island that already exists) and this fix is deliberately clear
   of it. `tests/island/mountains.test.ts` pins `mountainHexFor({q:1,r:0})` and
   `({q:2,r:0})`, the full weight list, and the 2763 count as the tripwire.

### The one consequence worth knowing

`keepOutFor(a, t)` answers "how wide is what stands on this rock hex" for the
walkability layer. A hex may now grow either model, and which one depends on its
neighbours, which `keepOutFor` cannot see. So it now returns the **maximum** of
the two — a conservative bound, not the true value. That is the safe direction:
under-reporting would open a gap the rules believe a pet can walk through and the
geometry does not. Over-report is at most 0.036 and changes no answer today,
because every mountain–mountain side is already shut by more than 0.05.

## PB-052 — a ring of six mountains sealed a pet in for good

### The defect

Six rock hexes around one grass hex. All six mountains place and none is refused.
At walking height the keep-outs are 1.03–1.06 against a 2.0000 spacing, so **every
consecutive pair OVERLAPS** and the ring is a closed wall. A pet of radius ZERO
cannot get out. There is no pathfinder, deliberately; `clearOf` is a hard
positional clamp and not a push, so it can never carry a pet through; the stuck
handler rerolls the GOAL and never the position. The pet lived in a disc of radius
~0.6 forever, and because island geometry is deterministic it survived reload. It
reads, accurately, as *"that bunny never goes anywhere"*.

The existing guarantee does not cover it. `coast.hasOutwardCorridor` walks EMPTY
CELLS — it is about where she can still BUILD, not where a pet can WALK — and rock
is explicitly exempt from it because rock can never cut a building corridor. True
for building, false for walking. That asymmetry is the whole hole.

### The ruling — this is the important part

A previous run measured all of this and proposed an elaborate fix: model rock
hexes as impassable cells in a new walkability layer plus a pet-side corridor
check that refuses the placement. **Joe ruled against that**, as JT-033, verbatim:

> **"C - just relocate the animal from a trapped position, that is no issue at all"**

So the requirement is **RECOVERY, NOT PREVENTION**. She may build the ring. No rock
tap silently becomes grass, no socket stops glowing, no topological invariant
needs proving, and **the walkability layer is out of scope**. If a friend ends up
inside a ring, the friend is moved.

### The fix, and why it is entirely in the pet layer

`flow.pets[].at` is the **hatch** hex and has never been written back in the life
of this game — a wandering pet's position lives only on the scene graph. So:

- **`src/island/world/walk.ts`** gained `rescueHexFor(...)`: given a hex, null if
  a pet there is not walled in (the common answer), otherwise the nearest
  reachable land hex. It prefers **open ground** — a rock hex IS its mountain, so
  putting a pet on the centre of one puts it inside the mesh — and breaks ties by
  coordinate so the answer is identical on every machine and every reload.
- **`src/island/pets.ts`** gained `rescueFrom` (memoised on the island object,
  which is safe because islands are immutable), `hexUnder`, and `openSpotOn`, and
  calls them in exactly two places:
  1. **`sync`**, where a pet is first put down. This is also the LOAD path, so an
     island that is already sealed repairs itself the next time she opens the
     game — and it is stateless, so no save changes shape and `pet.at` is never
     written.
  2. **the stuck handler**, for the pet that was already out walking when the
     sixth mountain landed. `sync` will not run again for a live pet, so this is
     the only code that can ever notice. It **asks** `rescueFrom` rather than
     counting rerolls: a pet merely wedged behind a tree gets the goal reroll it
     always got, and only a genuinely sealed pet is moved.
- **`src/island/flow.ts`** changed only comments. The old "REMEDY SEAM — held on
  JT-033, do not pick one" block now records which option was chosen and that
  options (a) and (b) are closed. `sealsAPet` and `sealedLand` stay exported and
  tested; they are the measurement and the tripwire, deliberately not guards.

### The free win

`firstFreeSpot` in `flow.ts` picks a hatch hex with no reachability check, so a
brand-new pet could be dealt a hex inside an existing pocket. Under recovery that
fixes itself — same question, same answer — so `firstFreeSpot` was deliberately
left alone rather than given a second remedy for a defect that already has one.

## How the two cards interact — read this before changing either

They are the same underlying fault (a mountain hex is terrain being placed
through the prop path), and fixing PB-053 **adds mountains where there were
none**, which can turn a ring that had a gap into a ring that does not. That is
fine, and it is why they had to ship together:

- `walk.ts`'s `keepOutFor` always assumed every rock hex had its mountain, so the
  sealing detection was already modelling the post-PB-053 world. The fix makes
  reality match the model rather than diverge from it.
- Any seal that PB-053's new mountains create is handled by PB-052's rescue.

Note also that the PB-053 fix makes PB-052's geometry *marginally* tighter: the
fallback family is narrower at placement size but WIDER at walking height. It
changes no answer today, and under recovery it does not need to.

## Tests

- **`tests/island/rescue.test.ts`** (new, 12 tests) — PB-052's remedy, driving the
  real `createPetField` with the real measured mountain keep-outs. It asserts the
  rescue fires, that it repairs a save on every load without writing `pet.at`,
  that the stuck handler frees a live wanderer, and — the point of the file —
  **that an ordinary pet on ordinary ground is NOT moved**. A rescue that fires on
  a healthy island would be a worse bug than the one being fixed. `Math.random`
  is stubbed with an LCG so the file is deterministic.
- **`tests/island/mountains.test.ts`** — the acceptance sweep: *2763 refusals, 0
  bare*, plus the fallback's purity, its derivation from the measured table, its
  disjointness from every refusable model, and the primary-chooser pins.
- **`tests/island/missing-model.test.ts`** — drives the REAL `props.sync` over two
  adjacent rock hexes and proves both get a peak. This closes a real gap: before
  this run, no test loaded mountain geometry through `createPropField` at all, so
  the refusal path had no end-to-end coverage.
- **`tests/island/rock.test.ts`** — the "one chooser" invariant is widened, not
  weakened, to "one primary chooser plus one fallback used only on refusal", and
  asserts `plot.ts` and `increments.ts` never touch the fallback.

**The revert probe was run.** With `rescueFrom` forced to return null, exactly the
four recovery assertions in `rescue.test.ts` fail and the eight negative ones stay
green — which is the right shape, and proves the tests measure the fix rather than
the mock.

## What I learned that is not in the code

- **`placed.add(k); continue` in `props.ts` is permanent for the session**, not
  for the frame. `placed` is never cleared and `createPropField` is constructed
  once in `main.ts`. Anything that "skips a hex for now" there is skipping it
  forever unless it deliberately avoids `placed.add`.
- **`flow.pets[].at` is hatch history, not position.** Three separate comments in
  the codebase say so and it is easy to disbelieve. Any fix that reads it as
  "where the animal is" is wrong; any fix that starts writing it is a first-ever
  write to a saved field.
- **`sync` skipping live pets is what forces two rescue call sites.** A pet
  already in `live` is never re-placed, so a change that only touches `sync`
  cannot help the pet that was walking when the wall closed.
- **The pet radius is not a dial.** Every gap on the island is either
  mountain-beside-mountain (already shut by 0.054–0.125) or has a mountain on one
  side only (0.937 wide) — and the fattest animal needs 0.38. There is no middle
  band, so "check against the real pet size" changes not one answer. Do not let
  anyone offer it as a compromise.
- **A hex lattice's cells are the Voronoi regions of their centres**, so "which
  hex is this point on" is just "nearest owned centre" — no inverse hex transform
  needed, and `hex.ts` deliberately does not have one.

## Anything still open

Nothing blocking. Two things a future run may want, both deliberately NOT done
here because they are outside these two cards:

1. `keepOutFor` is now a conservative bound rather than the true radius. Making it
   exact would mean giving it the island so it can see whether the fallback fired.
   Not worth it today — the over-report is 0.036 and changes no answer.
2. `firstFreeSpot` still hatches without a reachability check. Recovery covers it.
   If it is ever wanted as prevention it is one condition on one loop, and Joe
   said to ask him separately because where an animal appears is something a
   child sees.
